abstract: 'In pediatric chronic care, the triadic relationship among patients, caregivers, and healthcare providers introduces unique challenges for youth in managing their conditions. Diverging values, roles, and levels of situational awareness across decision-maker groups often hinder collaboration and affect health outcomes, highlighting the need to support collaborative decision-making. We conducted a co-design study with 19 participants (6 youth with chronic kidney disease, 6 caregivers, and 7 healthcare providers), exploring how digital technologies can support collaborative decision-making. Findings highlight that effective collaborative decision-making requires actively scaffolding team situational awareness across the patient–caregiver–clinician triad. We propose design considerations that surface and reconcile misaligned values by shifting emotional communication upstream, bridging clinical and home care through asynchronous tools, and deploying multi-user architectures that balance caregiver oversight with patient autonomy. This work provides generalizable design principles for socio-technical systems that empower family decision-making and promote shared understanding in pediatric chronic care and other multi-party healthcare contexts.'
