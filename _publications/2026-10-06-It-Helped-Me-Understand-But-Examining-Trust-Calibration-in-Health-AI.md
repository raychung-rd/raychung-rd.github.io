abstract: 'Health consumers and patients increasingly rely on AI for health guidance, yet how to support appropriate trust across different user groups remains unclear. We conducted a mixed-methods study with lay users (n = 194) and patients receiving dialysis (n = 19) to examine how trust signals, decision stakes, and lived disease-management experience shape trust in AI-generated nutrition advice. We found that citations and additional rationale increased trust, while lived disease-management experience was associated with lower overtrust but did not fully protect against misinformation. Prior knowledge supported trust calibration when it prompted verification but reinforced overtrust when existing beliefs were treated as evidence of correctness. We propose three design implications for consumer-facing health AI: designing for different trust-calibration needs across user groups, making transparency cues verifiable, and supporting reconciliation between AI advice and lived disease-management experience. Our work provides empirical evidence to inform the design of trustworthy health AI systems that support calibrated trust and informed human judgment in health decision-making.'
