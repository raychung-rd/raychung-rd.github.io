abstract: 'In pediatric chronic care, the triadic relationship among patients, caregivers, and clinicians introduces unique challenges for youth in managing their conditions. Diverging values, roles, and levels of situational awareness across decision-maker groups often hinder collaboration and affect health outcomes, highlighting the need to support collaborative decision-making. A mixed-methods study including a survey of 235 respondents and co-design workshops with 6 youth with chronic kidney disease, 6 caregivers, and 7 clinicians explored how digital technologies can be designed to support collaborative decision-making. Findings highlight the need for features that support information gathering, information comprehension, and shared-goal projection to build mutual understanding among decision-makers. We propose design principles balance caregiver support and youth autonomy development, prepare patients for clinician interactions, promote clinician awareness of patients’ readiness, and surface misaligned goals about next steps. This work advances the design of collaborative decision-making technologies that empower families and improve outcomes in pediatric chronic care.'
