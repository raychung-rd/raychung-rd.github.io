abstract: 'Patients increasingly rely on AI for health management, yet generic systems may lack clinical accountability and risk eroding patient–provider trust. We introduce AI expert proxies, clinician-authored agents that reflect an individual clinician''s practice philosophies, communication styles, and professional values. Through a survey (N = 48) and interviews (N = 6) with dietitians, we derived design requirements for proxy authoring and auditing and embodied them in ExTENDS, an AI-powered interactive system supporting non-technical domain experts in proxy creation and evaluation. Evaluations with dietitians (N = 12 system evaluation; N = 8 double-blind proxy evaluation) show that ExTENDS-authored proxies better reflect clinicians'' voice and clinical scope and inspire greater deployment confidence than baseline approaches. Our findings suggest that structured scaffolding supports proxy authoring while revealing a learning through delegation dynamic in which authoring promotes professional reflection. This work contributes to human–AI collaboration in healthcare by demonstrating how clinician-authored proxies can preserve clinician characteristics while extending asynchronous, personalized care.'
